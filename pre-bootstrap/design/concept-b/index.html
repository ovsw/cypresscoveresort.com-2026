You won’t have to lower your standards. Or your clothes.

Most nudist resorts are built to be nudist and little else: a pool, a clubhouse, and quiet by nine. Cypress Cove was built as a resort in 1964, and it has been run as one ever since.

  • Rooms, pools and restaurants

    Villa rooms, two heated pools, hot tubs, and three places to eat and drink on the grounds, including Cheeks Bar and Grill.

  • 300 acres and a 50-acre lake

    Palm walks, free boats on the lake, golf, pickleball, tennis and a spa. Enough space that it never feels crowded.

  • Open all year, with real service

    Central Florida weather and a staffed resort every day of the year. Themed weekends, live music and something on most evenings.

  • Everyone is vetted at the gate

    Photo ID for every guest, no cameras around the pools, and firm family-nudist standards. Not a public beach, and not a lifestyle resort.

Your first visit, step by step

No pressure to undress at once. Swimsuits are fine at the lake. No cameras anywhere. First-timer offer: a free tour, then a half-price day visit.

  1. 1

    Take a free tour

    Ask for a tour by phone or on the day. A staff member walks you around the grounds, clothed, and answers anything you want to ask.

  2. 2

    Come for a day

    Pay the day fee at the gate with photo ID. Stay as long as you like and go at your own pace.

  3. 3

    Stay the night

    A villa room or the campground. Book a weekend around an event, or a longer stay in winter.

Made for couples and for families

Two kinds of guests make up most of the resort. Both find the same thing: time together, with nothing to prove.

  • Couples: reconnect and unplug

    Quiet pools, the lake at sunset, dinner with no phone on the table. A private villa patio to come back to.

  • Families: wholesome, safe, judgment free

    Children are welcome and visit free. Every guest is vetted, cameras stay away, and the rules are the same for everyone.

Before you come

The five questions everyone asks before a first visit.

  • Will people stare?

    No. Staring is against the rules, and after the first hour most people forget anyone is undressed. You will feel more ordinary here than on a beach.

  • Do I have to undress at once?

    No. On a first visit you go at your own pace. Swimsuits are fine at the lake. The pools and hot tubs are nude.

  • Is it a sexual place?

    No. Cypress Cove is a family nudist resort. Sexual behaviour is not tolerated, every guest is vetted at the gate, and cameras stay away from the pools and the grounds.

  • Can I come on my own?

    Draft Single visitors are welcome under the singles policy. The exact wording is confirmed with the office and appears the same way everywhere on the site.

  • Can I bring the kids?

    Yes. Children are welcome and visit free with a parent. Families are a large part of the resort.
